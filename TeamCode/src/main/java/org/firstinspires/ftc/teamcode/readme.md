# Performance documentation for Hack Club: The Game.

![banner.png](https://fileshare.milesmuehlbach.com/public/media/robotics/Screenshot_20260706_222752.png)

This season has been a tumultuous one for us. For starters, it was my first ever season participating in First Tech Challenge. I was pretty much just expected to learn everything from the get-go which caused lots of hours, strife, and quite frankly underperforming robots early in the season.

After I got my stuff together, I'd say around January, I was still supposed to "stay in my lane" and only program autonomous. After advancing from our league tournament due to an award and *not* robot performance, we were determined to do well at the state tournament.

Certain circumstances with the robot build and the programming on the TeleOp side (at this time I wasn't allowed to touch the TeleOp code at all) resulted in, well, certainly caused us to *do*. I'm not sure if I would call it *well*.
35th place out of 36 places in the competition was certianly a way to go, but certianly not up.

Due to winning the inspire three award, we were yet again saved from the clutches of season ending and while we didn't advance to the world championship, we qualified to a "premier" event which is sorta like a pity event for people that don't make the world championship.

After this, the original programmer in charge of coding TeleOp and who was largely responsible for our performance at state, left, leaving me to do all the programming on the robot.

We told ourselves that we wouldn't be making any hardware changes to the robot, leaving purely all changes up to me.

Almost immediately, we noticed improvements. I rewrote the code "my way" and it was immediately more consistent, and much much faster. Combined with a new driver, and we felt very confident about our performance at the upcoming competition.

Now, just feeling good is no indicator for how we'll actually do. After many long hours spent working on the robot, we packed our bags and flew to Fort Worth, TX to attend the **Cowtown Invitational Premier Event**.

Improve was an understatement. Throughout our matches, we had demonstrable impact. While we didn't have the luckiest of schedules, our robot performance was shown by way of our OPR (Offensive power ranking). It's essentially a metric to objectively measure how "good" a team is over the other. During the state competition, our OPR was 30, which was in the 60th percentile for OPRs. During the event, our opr soared to 90, which is in the 90th percentile for OPRs!

Our good work didn't go unnoticed however, as despite us placing 25th out of 48, we showed that we were capable of being an alliance partner and were selected for an alliance to participate in the playoff matches! The top 16 teams play in playoffs.

Enough with my yapping though, here's the actual robot performance to prove it!
